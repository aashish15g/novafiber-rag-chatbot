## 1. Interpret targets correctly
This internal article is excluded from customer retrieval. Queue targets are fictional operational goals, not customer repair SLAs, compensation triggers, or evidence of a live ticket's status. Support intake is staffed 8 am-8 pm local service-address time daily. The network on-call function handles verified multi-service incidents continuously. Public installation and refund rules remain in the customer-approved documents; staff may not replace them with improvised promises.

## 2. P1 critical network incident
Assign P1 only after operations verifies a common network fault affecting at least 20 active residential services, a critical shared network node, or an urgent network infrastructure hazard requiring coordinated response. Multiple unverified neighbor reports alone are not confirmation. Network Operations owns P1 response, targets acknowledgment within 15 minutes on its continuous incident clock, and provides internal incident updates at least hourly until stabilization or a communicated change. For immediate life-safety danger, direct emergency action first; do not rely on a broadband ticket as emergency response.

## 3. P2 individual service outage
Assign P2 for an individual complete service loss after permitted basic checks, including persistent red LOS/PON faults not linked to a verified shared incident. Technical Support owns the case and targets an initial response within two support operating hours. An optical fault is not automatically P1. If reported outside 8 am-8 pm, the support-hour response clock begins at the next opening unless an on-call incident is established. Decide dispatch using NF-SP-003 rather than priority alone. No fixed resolution time is promised.

## 4. P3 degraded service and P4 administration
P3 covers repeatable speed degradation, intermittent service that is not a complete ongoing outage, and unresolved Wi-Fi faults. Technical Support targets initial response within one business day. Escalate intermittent service to P2 when a sustained complete outage is established. P4 covers ordinary account, plan, and billing administration; Customer Care/Billing targets acknowledgment within two business days. A billing dispute decision target is five business days after complete evidence under NF-BP-002, separate from the acknowledgment target.

## 5. Escalation triggers and ownership
If an acknowledgment/update target is missed, the queue owner escalates to the shift lead and records a new update commitment; do not silently restart clocks. Escalate repeated unresolved P2 symptoms, suspected shared faults, unsafe equipment, or contradictory remote observations to the technical lead. Escalate billing exceptions to the authorized billing owner; this article provides no unpublished credit limits. The receiving team must accept ownership in the ticket system, with a timestamp and next action. A chatbot answer never transfers ownership by itself.

## 6. Closure and customer-facing boundaries
Incident closure requires recorded operational validation; individual case closure requires service evidence or a documented account/billing decision. Communicate known facts and the next update without exposing internal queue details, individual staffing, or speculative causes. Actual priority, assigned technician, ETA, and SLA compliance are live operational facts, not values inferred from this static article.
